The oversight gap in weather modification
By Lucas Overby · Rainey Center

Cloud-seeding programs across the western states have expanded sharply since 2018. Wyoming, the most aggressive adopter, reports a 15% snowpack increase across treated basins.

Yet federal review of these efforts remains limited to a single 2021 GAO memo, leaving oversight almost entirely to the states that fund the programs.

Critics counter that downwind precipitation effects are well-documented in peer-reviewed literature, and that the absence of a national framework is itself the risk.

The states, for their part, argue the science is settled enough to act and that federal delay only widens the gap they are left to manage alone.
